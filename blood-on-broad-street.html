BLOOD ON
BROAD STREET

Before the blood, there was friendship. Three boys growing up in Lagos City never imagined that one ordinary day would become the beginning of a story that would change their lives forever.

The Story

BLOOD ON BROAD STREET is a Lagos City crime drama about friendship, survival, loyalty, betrayal and the consequences of choices made too early.

At the beginning, Tariq, Slim and Emeka are only ten-year-old boys trying to enjoy childhood in the middle of a busy Lagos neighborhood.

They have no money, no power and no idea what the future holds.

But they have something they value more than anything: each other.

"Before the blood... there was friendship."

Their story begins with something so simple that none of them could possibly understand what it would eventually become.

Episodes

Episode 1

The Three Boys

Scene 1 — Before the Blood

Lagos City — Late Afternoon

Lagos is alive.

Danfo buses push through traffic. Hawkers move between cars. Drivers argue over space. Somewhere nearby, music plays from a roadside shop while children chase one another through the street.

On a quieter stretch away from the main road, three ten-year-old boys stand together.

Tariq. Slim. Emeka.

Three boys.

One friendship.

No money. No power. No idea what tomorrow will bring.

But they have each other.

Tariq: You two are already making excuses before we start.
Slim: Excuses? For what? You know very well that I am going to beat you.
Emeka: Both of you talk too much. Just throw the stone.

Tariq bends down and picks up a small stone.

Tariq: Bet you can't throw yours farther than mine.
Slim: That's the easiest bet you have ever made.
Emeka: And when Tariq loses, nobody should let him change the rules.
Tariq: Me? Change the rules? Look at this face. Do I look like a cheater?
Slim: Yes.
Emeka: Completely.

They burst into laughter.

Slim throws first.

The stone flies through the air.

Slim: Beat that!

Emeka picks up his stone.

He takes a breath and throws.

The stone lands farther away.

Emeka: There. Now both of you can go home.
Tariq: You haven't won anything yet.

Tariq steps forward. He smiles.

Tariq: Watch carefully.

He throws.

The stone travels.

Farther.

Farther.

SPLASH!

The boys freeze for one second.

Slim: Ah!
Emeka: No way.
Tariq: Say it.
Slim: You got lucky.
Tariq: Say it properly.
Slim: Fine. You won.

Tariq raises both hands in victory.

Tariq: Thank you. Thank you. I would like to thank everybody who believed in me.

Emeka shakes his head while laughing.

Emeka: You are impossible.

For those few minutes, the world is simple.

They are only children.

And this is only a game.

Scene 2 — The Walk Home

Lagos City — Early Evening

The three friends walk along the roadside, kicking dust as they head home.

Slim: Tariq, one day you will get yourself into serious trouble.
Tariq: Me? I'm the most responsible person here.
Emeka: Responsible? You nearly climbed a moving bus last month.
Tariq: That was different.
Slim: How?
Tariq: I was testing my courage.
Emeka: You were testing whether your mother would beat you.

They laugh again.

They reach a junction and slow down.

Emeka: My mother told me to come straight home today.
Slim: Mine too.
Tariq: So? Are you both suddenly becoming obedient children?
Slim: I like my life.
Emeka: Same here.

Tariq smiles, then becomes quiet.

Tariq: Tomorrow, same place?
Emeka: Same place.
Slim: Unless you lose another bet.
Tariq: Never.

They bump fists.

Emeka: Three of us. Always.
Slim: Always.
Tariq: Always.

They separate and begin walking toward their homes.

None of them knows how important that promise will become.

Scene 3 — A Strange Encounter

Lagos City — Evening

Tariq is halfway home when he notices a dark vehicle parked near a small roadside shop.

He slows down.

Two grown men are standing beside the vehicle.

Their conversation is too low for him to understand.

Tariq watches for a moment.

Man 1: It should have been here already.
Man 2: Relax. It will come.
Man 1: We don't have time to relax.

Tariq hears a horn from the main road.

One of the men suddenly looks in his direction.

Tariq immediately turns away.

He walks faster.

He does not look back.

A few seconds later, the vehicle pulls away.

Tariq keeps walking.

He tells himself it was nothing.

Scene 4 — Tomorrow

Lagos City — Night

Night settles over the neighborhood.

The sounds of Lagos slowly change. The traffic becomes lighter. Shops begin to close.

Inside their separate homes, the three boys are unaware of each other's thoughts.

Somewhere in the same city, adults are making decisions that will eventually reach them.

Tariq lies in bed thinking about the afternoon.

Tariq: I really won that bet.

He smiles to himself.

Across the neighborhood, Slim is already asleep.

Emeka is being warned by his mother to stop spending so much time outside.

None of them understands why the ordinary day feels strangely unforgettable.

Scene 5 — The Promise

Lagos City — The Next Morning

Morning.

The streets come alive again.

One by one, the three boys return to the place where they spent the previous afternoon.

Tariq arrives first.

Slim arrives next.

Emeka arrives last.

Slim: You actually came.
Tariq: Of course. A champion never breaks his promise.
Emeka: Champion of what? Throwing stones?
Tariq: A champion is a champion.

They laugh.

The boys begin walking together.

Three friends.

One ordinary morning.

One city that is already moving toward something none of them can see.

They think they are walking into another normal day.

They are not.

Because somewhere ahead of them, their childhood is about to collide with the world of crime.

And when that happens, friendship will no longer be enough.

Episode 1

The Three Boys

Scene 1 — Before the Blood

Lagos City — Late Afternoon

The streets are alive. Hawkers call out to passing vehicles. Danfo buses move through traffic. Children play nearby while adults go about their business.

Three ten-year-old boys stand together on a quiet stretch near the roadside.

Tariq, Slim and Emeka have been friends for as long as they can remember.

They have no money. They have no power. They have no idea what the future will bring.

But they have each other.

Tariq: Look at you two. Both of you are scared already.
Slim: Scared of what? You? Please.
Emeka: Both of you talk too much. Just throw the stone.

Tariq picks up a small stone from the ground and studies it like a professional athlete.

Tariq: Bet you can't throw yours farther than mine.
Slim: That is the easiest bet you have ever made.
Emeka: Just don't cry when you lose.

The boys laugh.

One after another, they throw their stones.

The first stone flies.

The second goes farther.

Then Tariq takes his turn.

He throws.

The stone travels through the air...

SPLASH!

The boys stare toward the water.

Slim: Ha! I told you!
Emeka: You only got lucky.
Tariq: Lucky? Say it again.

They burst into laughter.

For a few minutes, they are just children.

Nothing else matters.

But none of them knows that the friendship they are celebrating today will eventually be tested by money, violence, secrets and blood.
