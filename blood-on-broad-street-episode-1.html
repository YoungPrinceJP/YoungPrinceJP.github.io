BLOOD ON BROAD STREET

Episode 1: Childhood Beginnings

Before the rivalry, before the betrayal, there was friendship.

Scene 1 — The Three Boys

[Lagos City. Late afternoon. Three ten-year-old friends, Tariq, Slim and Emeka, play beside a canal, competing to see who can throw a stone the farthest.]

Slim: You people should stop celebrating small victories. Let us throw again, and this time nobody should start making excuses when I win.

Emeka: You have been saying that since we started. Maybe the problem is not the stones or the way you throw them. Maybe you simply need to practise.

Tariq: Both of you should calm down. It is a game, not a fight. Let us see who actually throws the farthest.

[Emeka's stone lands farther than the others. Their laughter is interrupted when Boyo, an older neighbourhood boy, approaches and pushes Slim into the canal.]

Slim: Boyo! What is wrong with you? You think you can push me around whenever you feel like it?

Tariq: Slim, come out first. Don't let him decide what you do next. He wants you to lose your temper so he can laugh at you again.

Emeka: Tariq is right. We cannot solve every problem by fighting. We need to understand why Boyo behaves this way and find a smarter way to deal with him.

Tariq: We don't fight Boyo. We outsmart him.

Before the blood... there was friendship.

Scene 2 — The Plan Begins

[A quiet corner behind the neighbourhood shops. Late afternoon. The three friends discuss what happened at the canal.]

Tariq: If we want this to stop, we must understand Boyo instead of giving him the reaction he expects. We should watch what he does and think before we act.

Emeka: Have you noticed that he acts bold around younger children but becomes quiet whenever older boys appear? That tells us something about him.

Slim: I understand the plan, but I still don't like letting him believe he can do anything he wants. I just don't want us to become afraid of him.

[Mama Ngozi, a roadside shopkeeper, warns them not to let another person's bad behaviour turn them into troublemakers. The boys agree to observe Boyo rather than rush into a fight.]

Scene 3 — The Neighbourhood Has Eyes

[The next morning, on a busy Lagos street. The boys spot Boyo with two older youths: one wearing a faded football jersey, the other with a scar above his eyebrow.]

Emeka: Look at Boyo. He doesn't seem as confident with those two as he does when he is troubling younger children.

Tariq: Don't stare at them for too long. We don't know who they are or what they want from him. Let's watch quietly and keep moving.

[The older youths pressure Boyo to bring money. Boyo notices the three friends watching and challenges them. They leave without confronting the group. The thin-faced youth watches them go and tells his companion to keep an eye on them.]

Every street has its own rules. Some are learned the hard way.

Scene 4 — A Promise Between Friends

[A public primary school in Lagos City, late morning. The boys talk quietly before class.]

Tariq: Standing up for yourself doesn't always mean fighting. Sometimes it means having the courage to do the right thing even when someone wants to provoke you.

Emeka: And friendship means telling each other the truth, even when we don't want to hear it. If one of us is making a mistake, the others should speak up.

Slim: Then let's make a promise. We won't deliberately put one another in danger, we will be honest with each other, and we won't abandon a friend just because things become difficult.

[Their teacher, Mr. Adeyemi, catches them arriving late and jokes that they can make up for it by answering questions on the board. The boys return to class, still laughing.]

Scene 5 — The Trouble at the School Gate

[Afternoon, outside the school gate. The three friends see Boyo threatening Chidi, a younger Primary Five pupil, and demanding money while holding on to his schoolbag.]

Slim: We cannot just walk past him while he is doing this to Chidi. We know what it feels like when someone bigger thinks they can frighten you into doing whatever they want.

Emeka: We promised not to put one another in danger. We should be careful, but that doesn't mean we have to pretend we didn't see anything.

Tariq: Boyo, let him go. There is no reason to take his money or hold his bag. If you have a problem with him, speak to a teacher instead of frightening him.

[The football-jersey youth arrives and orders Boyo to stop attracting attention and come along. Boyo releases Chidi but warns the trio that helping others will bring trouble.]

Tariq: Chidi, tell your family and your teacher what happened. You shouldn't have to handle this alone, and you should ask an adult for help if he comes after you again.

Emeka: Boyo may think we are interfering now. We need to be ready for that, because doing the right thing doesn't mean there will never be consequences.

Slim: I know. But I don't want to become the kind of person who watches someone being bullied and walks away just because helping might cause trouble.

[Across the street, the two older youths watch the boys leave. The one in the football jersey warns his companion that children who interfere may become a problem later.]
